Solving linear systems

The plan. One method, row reduction, solves every linear system. You only have to learn to read the answer off the reduced matrix. There are three possible outcomes: exactly one solution, infinitely many, or none. Each example below was solved step by step by GoatVex and checked by the verifier.

1. One system, three ways to write it

Take this system of equations (Example A below):

$$\begin{aligned}x + y + z &= 6\\2 y + 5 z &= -4\\2 x + 5 y - z &= 27\end{aligned}$$

Matrix equation (Nicholson 2.2): put the coefficients in a matrix A, the unknowns in a column x, and the right-hand sides in b:

$$\left[\begin{matrix}1 & 1 & 1\\0 & 2 & 5\\2 & 5 & -1\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}x\\y\\z\end{matrix}\right] = \left[\begin{matrix}6\\-4\\27\end{matrix}\right]$$

Vector equation: read A column by column. The system asks which combination of the columns of A gives b:

$$x\left[\begin{matrix}1\\0\\2\end{matrix}\right] + y\left[\begin{matrix}1\\2\\5\end{matrix}\right] + z\left[\begin{matrix}1\\5\\-1\end{matrix}\right] = \left[\begin{matrix}6\\-4\\27\end{matrix}\right]$$

Augmented matrix [A | b]: the version you row reduce:

$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 2 & 5 & -4 \\ 2 & 5 & -1 & 27\end{array}\right]$$
All four forms are the same problem. That's why "is b a linear combination of the columns?", "is b in the span of the columns?" and "does Ax = b have a solution?" are all the same question.

2. The picture: what can happen

Each equation in three unknowns is a plane in 3D space. A solution is a point that lies on every plane at once.

  • Exactly one solution: the planes meet at a single point.
  • Infinitely many: the planes share a whole line (or plane). The solutions are described with free parameters like t.
  • No solution (inconsistent): there's no point common to all of them, e.g. two parallel planes, or three planes that meet in pairs but never all together.

Two equations in two unknowns are two lines. They meet once, coincide, or are parallel. It's the same three cases.

3. The method: row reduce [A | b] to RREF

The three elementary row operations never change the solutions:

  • Swap two rows: R₁ ↔ R₂
  • Scale a row by a nonzero number: R₂ → (1/2)R₂
  • Replace a row by itself plus a multiple of another: R₃ → R₃ − 2R₁

Nicholson's Gaussian algorithm: get a leading 1 in the first column, make zeros below it, move to the next column, and repeat. That gives row echelon form. Then clear the entries above each leading 1, working from the right, to reach reduced row echelon form (RREF).

How to read the RREF
  1. A row [0 0 … 0 | c] with c ≠ 0 means 0 = c. That's impossible, so there is no solution.
  2. Otherwise, if every variable column has a leading 1 (pivot), there is exactly one solution.
  3. Otherwise, the columns without a pivot are free variables. Each gets a parameter (t, s, …), and there are infinitely many solutions.

4. Example A: exactly one solution

$$\begin{aligned}x + y + z &= 6\\2 y + 5 z &= -4\\2 x + 5 y - z &= 27\end{aligned}$$
1R₃ → R₃ − 2R₁
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 2 & 5 & -4 \\ 2 & 5 & -1 & 27\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 2 & 5 & -4 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 15\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 1.
2R₂ → (1/2)R₂
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 2 & 5 & -4 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 15\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{5}{2} & -2 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 15\end{array}\right]$$
Scale row 2 so the pivot in column 2 becomes a leading 1.
3R₃ → R₃ − 3R₂
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{5}{2} & -2 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 15\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{5}{2} & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & - \frac{21}{2} & 21\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 2.
4R₃ → (−2/21)R₃
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{5}{2} & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & - \frac{21}{2} & 21\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{5}{2} & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -2\end{array}\right]$$
Scale row 3 so the pivot in column 3 becomes a leading 1.
5R₁ → R₁ − R₃
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{5}{2} & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -2\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 0 & 8 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{5}{2} & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -2\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero above the leading 1 in column 3.
6R₂ → R₂ − (5/2)R₃
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 0 & 8 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{5}{2} & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -2\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 0 & 8 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -2\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero above the leading 1 in column 3.
7R₁ → R₁ − R₂
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 0 & 8 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -2\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 0 & 0 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -2\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero above the leading 1 in column 2.
Read off the answer
$$\mathbf{x} = \left[\begin{matrix}5\\3\\-2\end{matrix}\right]$$

Every variable column has a pivot, so there is exactly one solution: x = 5, y = 3, z = −2.

5. Example B: infinitely many solutions (vector form)

$$\begin{aligned}x - y + z &= 1\\2 x + y - z &= 5\\3 x &= 6\end{aligned}$$
1R₂ → R₂ − 2R₁
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 & 5 \\ 3 & 0 & 0 & 6\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 3 \\ 3 & 0 & 0 & 6\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 1.
2R₃ → R₃ − 3R₁
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 3 \\ 3 & 0 & 0 & 6\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 3\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 1.
3R₂ → (1/3)R₂
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 3\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 3\end{array}\right]$$
Scale row 2 so the pivot in column 2 becomes a leading 1.
4R₃ → R₃ − 3R₂
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & -3 & 3\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 2.
5R₁ → R₁ + R₂
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 0 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero above the leading 1 in column 2.
Read off the answer

Column 3 has no pivot, so z is free: set z = t. The RREF rows say x = 2 and y − z = 1, so y = 1 + t. As one vector, the general solution is

$$\mathbf{x} = \left[\begin{matrix}2\\t + 1\\t\end{matrix}\right] = \left[\begin{matrix}2\\1\\0\end{matrix}\right] + t\left[\begin{matrix}0\\1\\1\end{matrix}\right],\qquad t \in \mathbb{R}$$

This is the parametric vector form: one particular solution plus t times a direction vector. Geometrically it's a line in 3D: all the points where the three planes meet. Every value of t gives a solution (t = 0 gives (2, 1, 0)).

6. Example C: no solution

$$\begin{aligned}x + y + z &= 1\\x + 2 y + 3 z &= 2\\2 x + 3 y + 4 z &= 4\end{aligned}$$
1R₂ → R₂ − R₁
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 & 4\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 & 4\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 1.
2R₃ → R₃ − 2R₁
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 & 4\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 2\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 1.
3R₃ → R₃ − R₂
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 2\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 2.
4R₁ → R₁ − R₃
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero above the leading 1 in column 4.
5R₂ → R₂ − R₃
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero above the leading 1 in column 4.
6R₁ → R₁ − R₂
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero above the leading 1 in column 2.
Read off the answer

Row 3 of the RREF is [0 0 0 | 1], which says 0x + 0y + 0z = 1, i.e. 0 = 1. That can never be true, so the system is inconsistent: no solution. You can stop as soon as a row like this appears; there's no need to finish the RREF.

7. Example D: a homogeneous system (b = 0)

When every right-hand side is 0, x = 0 is always a solution, so a homogeneous system is never inconsistent. The only question is whether there are other solutions.

$$\begin{aligned}x_{1} + 2 x_{2} - x_{3} &= 0\\2 x_{1} + 4 x_{2} - 2 x_{3} &= 0\end{aligned}$$
1R₂ → R₂ − 2R₁
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 2 & 4 & -2 & 0\end{array}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{array}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 1.
Read off the answer

Only column 1 has a pivot, so x₂ = s and x₃ = t are free. Row 1 says x₁ = −2s + t. In vector form:

$$\mathbf{x} = s\left[\begin{matrix}-2\\1\\0\end{matrix}\right] + t\left[\begin{matrix}1\\0\\1\end{matrix}\right],\qquad s, t \in \mathbb{R}$$

Two free variables give two direction vectors, so the solution set is a plane through the origin: the span of those two vectors. This is the null space of the coefficient matrix. Compare with Example B: the solutions of Ax = b are always one particular solution plus the solutions of Ax = 0.

8. Example E: the vector form in reverse (span)

Is w = (2, 5, 8) a linear combination of v₁ = (1, 2, 3) and v₂ = (0, 1, 2)? This asks: do numbers c₁, c₂ exist with c₁v₁ + c₂v₂ = w? That's a system with augmented matrix [v₁ v₂ | w].

1R₂ → R₂ − 2R₁
$$\left[\begin{matrix}1 & 0 & 2\\2 & 1 & 5\\3 & 2 & 8\end{matrix}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{matrix}1 & 0 & 2\\0 & 1 & 1\\3 & 2 & 8\end{matrix}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 1.
2R₃ → R₃ − 3R₁
$$\left[\begin{matrix}1 & 0 & 2\\0 & 1 & 1\\3 & 2 & 8\end{matrix}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{matrix}1 & 0 & 2\\0 & 1 & 1\\0 & 2 & 2\end{matrix}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 1.
3R₃ → R₃ − 2R₂
$$\left[\begin{matrix}1 & 0 & 2\\0 & 1 & 1\\0 & 2 & 2\end{matrix}\right]\;\longrightarrow\;\left[\begin{matrix}1 & 0 & 2\\0 & 1 & 1\\0 & 0 & 0\end{matrix}\right]$$
Create a zero below the leading 1 in column 2.
4Write w as a combination
$$\left[\begin{matrix}2\\5\\8\end{matrix}\right]$$
With every free coefficient set to 0, the RREF gives c₁ = 2, c₂ = 1.
Read off the answer

No row reads 0 = nonzero, so the system is consistent and w is in the span. The RREF gives c₁ = 2, c₂ = 1:

$$\left[\begin{matrix}2\\5\\8\end{matrix}\right] = 2\left[\begin{matrix}1\\2\\3\end{matrix}\right] + 1\left[\begin{matrix}0\\1\\2\end{matrix}\right]$$

9. Recap

  1. Write the augmented matrix [A | b]. The same system can be written as equations, as Ax = b, or as a vector equation x₁a₁ + … + xₙaₙ = b.
  2. Row reduce with the Gaussian algorithm: leading 1, zeros below, next column, then zeros above.
  3. Read the RREF: a row 0 = c (c ≠ 0) means no solution; a pivot in every variable column means one solution; any free variable means infinitely many.
  4. Infinitely many: set each free variable equal to a parameter, solve for the pivot variables, and write x = p + t·v (+ s·u …).
  5. Homogeneous (b = 0): always consistent, and the solution set is a span (the null space).

Textbook: Nicholson §1.1–1.3 (systems, Gaussian elimination), §1.6 (applications), §2.2 (matrix form); Poole §2.1–2.2.
